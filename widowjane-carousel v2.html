01 / Origin
How it began

It started with chocolate, not whisky.

Widow Jane grew out of Cacao Prieto, a bean-to-bar chocolate operation in Red Hook. Same building, same obsession with fermentation, moved from cocoa to rum to whisky.

@DineDrinkRepeat02 / 10
02 / Water
The name

The mine is more than a name.

Widow Jane is named after a decommissioned limestone mine in Rosendale, New York. Its mineral-rich water is brought to Brooklyn and used to proof every batch.

Proofing = bringing whisky down to bottling strength with water.

03 / Blending
The education slide

Not every Widow Jane bourbon starts in Brooklyn.

The flagship 10 Year brings together straight bourbons from Kentucky, Tennessee and Indiana, selected and married in Brooklyn five barrels at a time, then proofed with Rosendale water.

Sourced doesn’t mean bought-and-labelled. The selecting, blending and proofing is the craft.

04 / Corn
But they distil too

They grow their own corn.

Alongside the blend, Widow Jane distils its own estate whisky, and Baby Jane is made from a heirloom corn they cross-pollinated in-house. A grain no one else can plant.

06 / Taste
The one that explains it

The 10 Year explains the brand best.

Deep and rich, wood and dark chocolate over stone fruit and baking spice. Classic bourbon, done right. (The tour opened with a chamomile-infused rye cocktail shot, a nice touch.)

The takeaway
10 / 10

Brooklyn doesn’t make this bourbon interesting. Everything around it does.

Sourced barrels, New York distilling, proprietary corn, mine water, blending, finishing, constant experimentation. A decade in, and big enough that the giants came knocking.

Save this for your next New York whisky crawl. 🥃
