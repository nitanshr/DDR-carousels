Red Hook, Brooklyn
Widow Jane

The New York whisky standing up to Kentucky.

Barely a decade old, and one of the new-wave American names I most wanted to see up close. So I visited the distillery.

How it began
02 / 08

It started as a chocolate factory.

Widow Jane grew out of Cacao Prieto, a bean-to-bar chocolate operation in Red Hook. The same building, the same fermentation obsession, moved from cocoa to rum to whisky.

@DineDrinkRepeat02 / 08
The name
03 / 08

Named after its water.

The brand takes its name from the Widow Jane Mine, a decommissioned limestone quarry in Rosendale, New York. Its mineral-rich, limestone-filtered water is what every batch is proofed with.

That same Rosendale limestone built parts of the Statue of Liberty and landmarks across New York.

@DineDrinkRepeat03 / 08
What makes it theirs
04 / 08

Water and corn no one else can use.

Their own limestone-mine water. Their own heirloom corn, cross-pollinated in-house for Baby Jane. And a blend married by hand, five barrels at a time. Provenance you genuinely cannot copy.

@DineDrinkRepeat04 / 08
How it’s made
05 / 08

A blender and a distiller.

The flagship 10-year is sourced straight bourbon from Kentucky, Indiana and Tennessee, blended in Brooklyn and cut with the mine water. That blending program is what put Widow Jane on the map.

Alongside it, they distill their own estate whisky in small 10-gallon barrels. Non-chill-filtered, small, hands-on.

At the distillery
06 / 08

First pours, on the distillery floor.

The tour started with a chamomile-infused rye cocktail shot, then the 10-year itself: deep, rich, wood and dark chocolate and stone fruit. Classic bourbon, done right.

The takeaway
08 / 08
Widow Jane

Corn no one can plant. Water no one can use. A taste that’s unmistakably its own.

Barely a decade in, Widow Jane went from a Brooklyn chocolate factory to a name big enough that America’s whisky giants came knocking. Worth raising this Bourbon Heritage Month. Save this for your New York list.
